The Triadic Renormalization Group

In this section we introduce the Triadic Renormalization Group (TRG): a formal recursion group acting on triadic state vectors associated with closure–boundary systems. Definition of the Triadic Renormalization Group

Scope, Limitations, and Ecosystem Positioning

Conceptual Scope and Limitations

Triadic Renormalization (TRG) provides a structural recursion framework for closure–boundary systems. Its purpose is architectural rather than dynamical: TRG formalizes how informational, energetic, and geometric roles reorganize under recursive normalization, and how this reorganization induces material time. Several limitations follow naturally from this scope:

  • No microscopic dynamics. TRG does not specify microphysical laws for I, E, or G. It formalizes their recursive reorganization but does not derive their internal evolution from first principles.

  • Dual time as scaling, not law. The relation between tmat and tEM is a closure-dependent scaling function F(In,En,Gn), not a universal dynamical equation. Multiplicative forms such as (1+αS) f(Meff,I) are structural ansätze rather than exact physical laws.

  • Black-hole scaling is a special case. The Hawking-calibrated behaviour dtmat/dtEM ∝ 1/M2 applies only to horizon-bounded closures where mass dominates effective damping and evaporation provides the relevant beta-function. It is not a general closure law.

  • No claim of empirical completeness. TRG offers structural interpretations of phenomena such as slow spacecraft aging or early-universe maturity, but these interpretations are hypothesis-level and not presented as definitive explanations.

  • Coarse-graining, not prediction engine. TRG organizes closure dynamics into a renormalization flow; it does not compute detailed time-dependent behaviour of specific physical systems.

These limitations reflect the intended role of TRG: a structural grammar for closure recursion rather than a replacement for established physical theories.
