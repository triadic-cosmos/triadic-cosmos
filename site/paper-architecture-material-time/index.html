This paper develops five structural theorems that together define the behaviour of material time and its relationship to entropy, recursion, mass, and electromagnetic processes. They also highlight the need for quantitative formalization, experimental validation, and integration with existing physical theories.

Conclusion

The Triadic Cosmos framework provides a unified temporal ontology in which entropy, recursion index, mass, and dual time jointly determine the evolution, stability, and lifetime of physical closures across all scales. The five structural theorems developed in this work establish that aging, material lifetime, and temporal flow are not domain‑specific phenomena but universal properties of entropic closures. Biological organisms, chemical systems, planetary bodies, stars, neutron stars, and horizon‑bounded objects all obey the same structural laws.

The first theorem shows that aging is the entropic acceleration of material time: as entropy accumulates, the recursion index rises and the internal temporal flow of the closure accelerates. The second theorem demonstrates that this process is structurally inevitable for any active closure. Entropy production is inseparable from participation in the triadic cycle, and aging can be slowed but never eliminated without halting recursion entirely.

The third theorem reveals that material lifetime is a monotonic function of entropy. High‑entropy closures evolve rapidly and possess short lifetimes, while low‑entropy closures evolve slowly and can persist for cosmological durations. This principle explains the rapid evolution of massive stars, the moderate aging of biological organisms, and the near‑frozen temporal behaviour of degenerate matter.

The fourth theorem introduces mass‑scaled entropic recursion: the rate at which entropy increases recursion index is inversely proportional to mass. High‑mass closures experience large entropic increments and rapid recursion acceleration, while low‑mass closures experience small increments and slow acceleration. This mass‑scaled law explains why massive stars evolve quickly, why humans age gradually, why microscopic organisms age extremely slowly, and why large dinosaurs exhibited long lifespans.

The fifth theorem establishes the independence of aging from electromagnetic time. EM processes evolve under tEM and can influence material closures only indirectly through energy transfer or structural change. Aging itself remains a purely material‑time phenomenon governed by entropy and recursion index.

Together, these theorems reveal that temporal behaviour across the cosmos is governed by a single structural principle: entropy drives recursion, recursion shapes material time, and mass modulates the rate of entropic impact. Aging, evolution, stability, and collapse are therefore unified expressions of the same triadic architecture. The resulting ontology provides a coherent, predictive, and empirically grounded interpretation of cross‑domain temporal phenomena, linking biological aging, planetary cooling, chemical maturation, stellar evolution, neutron‑star longevity, and horizon‑bounded dynamics under one universal temporal framework.
