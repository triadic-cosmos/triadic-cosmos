The Triadic Ontology: Energy, Geometry, Information

Modern physics contains many successful theories, but all of them instantiate the same three irreducible structural roles. The Triadic Ontology identifies these roles—energy, geometry, and information—as the minimal architectural basis from which all physical law emerges. They are not microscopic entities but invariant structural functions that every viable physical theory must realize.

Energy (E)

Energy represents the dynamical and causal resources of a physical system. It includes stress–energy, Hamiltonian content, excitations, thermodynamic flux, and computational capacity. In the triadic architecture, energy plays the dynamical role: it drives change, mediates interactions, and determines how geometric and informational structures evolve.

  • In GR, energy sources curvature through the Einstein equations.

  • In QFT, energetic excitations propagate through geometric structure.

  • In thermodynamics, informational change has energetic cost (Landauer-type relations).

  • In quantum information, energetic bounds determine the rate of state change (quantum speed limits).

Energy therefore constrains geometry and is constrained by information, forming the I → E leg of the triadic cycle.

Geometry (G)

Geometry encodes causal structure, curvature, horizon formation, and the spatial–temporal relations that determine which processes are accessible. In the triadic architecture, geometry plays the constraint role: it bounds informational capacity and regulates energetic evolution.

  • In GR, geometry determines causal accessibility and horizon structure.

  • In holography, geometric area bounds informational capacity (Bekenstein, RT/HRT).

  • In EP=EPR, geometric connectivity corresponds to entanglement structure.

  • In closure cosmology, geometry defines the horizon-bounded domain of a closure.

Geometry therefore constrains information and is shaped by energy, forming the E → G leg of the triadic cycle.

Information (I)

Information represents distinguishability, entropy, entanglement, computational structure, and holographic encoding. In the triadic architecture, information plays the generative role: it creates energetic evolution, reorganizes geometric structure, and determines the accessible state space of a closure.

  • In QM, informational structure determines unitary evolution.

  • In holography, entanglement patterns generate bulk geometry.

  • In thermodynamics, entropy governs irreversibility and emergent temporal flow.

  • In RG flow, informational coarse-graining defines recursion.

Information therefore generates energy and is bounded by geometry, forming the G → I leg of the triadic cycle.

Irreducibility of the Triadic Roles

The triadic roles are irreducible: none can be derived from the others, and no physical theory can omit any of them without becoming architecturally incomplete. Attempts to quantize geometry, derive information from energy, or treat energy as emergent from geometry all fail because they collapse one structural role into another. The success of modern physics arises precisely because its major pillars each instantiate different legs of the triadic cycle.

Triadic Interdependence

E → G → I → E,

This cycle is not optional: any violation—negative energy, non-holographic information, or geometry without informational capacity—breaks recursion, halts emergent time, and renders a structure physically inadmissible.
