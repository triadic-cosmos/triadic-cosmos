Introduction

The Triadic Cosmos ecosystem provides such a principle. This dual–time architecture implies that closures normalize toward a boundary canon that is effectively timeless. As a result, closure states may exhibit anticipatory structure that appears retro–causal when interpreted within a single–time ontology.

This paper develops the consequences of dual–time closure–boundary dynamics for the interpretation of retro–causal phenomena. We introduce future–consistency normalization: the process by which a closure reorganizes its internal geometry to align with a boundary canon that does not evolve in material time. Because the boundary canon is time–independent, the closure’s recursive evolution can produce states that appear influenced by future boundary projections, even though no physical time reversal occurs. Retro–causality is therefore not a fundamental feature of nature, but an emergent property of systems whose evolution is governed by dual temporal scales and holographic projection.

By situating these phenomena within a single dual–time closure–boundary architecture, this paper argues that retro–causal signatures across physics, cognition, and artificial systems are not paradoxes but structural consequences of future–consistency normalization. What Retro‑Causality Is

Retro–causality refers to physical situations in which future boundary conditions appear to influence present closure states. In standard single–time ontologies, such behaviour is paradoxical: causal influence is assumed to propagate forward in time, and any apparent dependence of present events on future choices is interpreted as a violation of temporal order. Retro–causal signatures therefore occupy a conceptual grey zone in modern physics, appearing in quantum experiments, holographic dualities, cosmological evolution, and cognitive processing, yet lacking a coherent structural explanation.

The classical interpretation of retro–causality relies on three problematic assumptions. First, it presumes a single universal time shared by matter, fields, observers, and the cosmos. Second, it assumes that causal structure is globally defined rather than horizon–bounded and closure–dependent. Third, it treats boundary conditions as temporally embedded rather than as time–invariant constraints. Within this framework, retro–causal phenomena seem to imply either time reversal, determinism, or block– universe ontology. Each of these interpretations conflicts with empirical behaviour and with the architectural principles established in holographic and emergent frameworks.

A more precise characterization of retro–causality arises from holographic systems. In AdS/CFT, bulk events can be reconstructed from boundary data that is specified at later times, producing apparent violations of bulk causal order. In delayed–choice quantum–eraser experiments, interference patterns depend on measurement settings chosen after the photons have been detected. In cosmology, bulk matter evolves toward attractor configurations determined by boundary fields that do not evolve in material time. In cognitive science, predictive processing shows that perceptual content is shaped by future–oriented priors rather than by past sensory input alone. These examples share a common structure: the apparent retro–causal influence arises from a mismatch between the temporal layer of the closure and the temporal layer of the boundary.

Within the Triadic Cosmos, retro–causality is reinterpreted as a structural consequence of dual–time closure–boundary dynamics. As a result, closure evolution constitutes a recursive normalization toward a boundary canon that does not change in material time. This produces the appearance that future boundary conditions influence present closure states, even though no physical time reversal occurs.

Retro–causality is therefore not a violation of causal order but an emergent property of systems whose evolution is governed by dual temporal scales. It arises whenever a finite closure reorganizes its internal geometry to align with a time–invariant boundary canon. The apparent influence of future events is reinterpreted as future–consistency normalization: the closure’s attempt to minimize divergence from a boundary structure that is fixed in tEM but encountered recursively in tmat.

This structural interpretation dissolves the paradoxes associated with retro–causality. It requires no determinism, no block–universe ontology, and no reversal of temporal flow. Instead, it reveals that retro–causal signatures are the natural behaviour of dual–time closure–boundary systems, appearing whenever emergent material time interacts with a timeless holographic boundary.
